Rigs

Two-hook flapper (crimps & beads)

WhitingDabsFlounderSole
  • Body: 60–80lb mono, about 1.2m long.
  • Snoods: 20–30lb mono, shorter than the gap between the stations so they can't tangle.
  • Range: short to medium. This is your everyday winter rig.

Clipped-down rig

DistanceRaysCodlingInto a headwind
  • Each snood hooks onto a bait clip just above the lead. The baits sit behind the lead, so they fly like a dart.
  • When the lead hits the water, the snoods pop free.
  • Good for long casts, soft baits and wind in your face.

Pennell / bomber (big bait)

CodBig whitingRays
  • Use two 2/0–3/0 hooks on one snood, so a long bait is held at both ends.
  • It casts a big bait without it sliding down into a ball.
  • Use it on the evening ebb at Frinton and Walton, or after a blow anywhere.

Pulley rig

SmoothhoundRaysRough ground
  • The main line runs through a bead or swivel to the lead. Under strain, the lead lifts up and over snags.
  • It gives strong hook-holds on big fish and fewer lost leads on rough ground.

Running ledger

BassEelsFlounderLight tide
  • The line slides freely through the lead, so a fish feels no weight and takes the bait confidently.
  • Use it close in, in light tide, with a single hook and a natural bait.

Bait presentation

The golden rule: elastic should hold the bait, not squeeze it. Mushy lug almost always comes from winding too tight or from overly wet, soft worms. See the elastic card.

Elastic: hold, don't squeeze

  • Gut and dry your lug before you go. Wrap them in dry newspaper overnight in the fridge and they firm up a lot.
  • Pull the elastic off the spool with no stretch. Let the spiral lie on the bait, and only tension it when you lock off on the snood.
  • Fewer, wider turns: 4–6 turns over a 10cm bait is plenty.
  • Too soft anyway? Use a squid sleeve and put the pressure on the squid, not the worm.
  • Use thin elastic cotton, not thick shirring elastic.
