How to tie it

    Bait presentation

    The golden rule: elastic should hold the bait, not squeeze it. Mushy lug comes from winding too tight, or from wet, ungutted worms.

    Elastic: hold, don't squeeze

    • Gut and dry your lug before you go. A night in dry newspaper in the fridge firms them right up.
    • Pull the elastic off the spool with no stretch and let the spirals lie on the bait. Only tension it when you lock off on the snood.
    • Use fewer, wider turns. 4–6 turns over a 10cm bait is plenty.
    • Still too soft? Add a squid sleeve and put the pressure on the squid, not the worm.
    • Use thin elastic cotton, not thick shirring elastic.

    Knots

    5-turn grinner (uni knot)

    Swivels, lead links, snoods to swivels.

    1. Pass the line through the eye and double it back about 15cm alongside the main line.
    2. Bring the tag end back toward the eye to form a loop lying over the doubled line.
    3. Wrap the tag 5 times around both lines, inside the loop, working away from the eye.
    4. Wet it, pull the tag to close the turns, then pull the main line so the knot slides down tight to the eye.
    5. Trim the tag to 3mm.

    Snood knot

    Hooks. The line pulls straight off the shank.

    1. Pass the line through the eye from the front and lay 5cm of tag along the shank toward the bend.
    2. Hold the tag against the shank, and wrap the line from the eye side tightly around shank and tag 7 times, working toward the bend.
    3. Pass the line back through the eye from the back.
    4. Wet it and pull the main line while holding the hook. The turns bed down neatly. Trim the tag.

    Dropper loop

    Snood loops on knotted flappers.

    1. Form a 10cm loop in the rig body where the dropper should be.
    2. Take one side of the loop and twist it around the other 6–7 times. A gap opens in the middle of the twists.
    3. Push the bottom of the loop up through that middle gap and hold it with a finger.
    4. Wet it, then pull both ends of the main line apart. The twists roll together and the loop stands out at 90°. Aim for a finished loop 2–3cm long.

    Surgeon's loop

    A loop on the end of a snood.

    1. Double the end of the line about 10cm.
    2. Tie an overhand knot with the doubled line.
    3. Pass the loop through the overhand knot a second time.
    4. Wet it and pull the loop and both lines to tighten. Trim the tag.

    Loop-to-loop

    Attaching snoods to dropper loops.

    1. Push the snood's end loop through the dropper loop.
    2. Pass the whole snood (hook first) back through its own loop.
    3. Pull it slowly so the two loops lock like a reef knot, not girth-hitched in a strangled loop.

    Crimping

    Fixing stations, bait clips and stops.

    1. Match the crimp to the line: 1.2mm for 50–60lb and 1.4mm for 80lb.
    2. Slide it into position, butted against its bead or clip.
    3. Seat it in the middle of the crimping pliers' jaws and give it one firm squeeze.
    4. Tug-test it. If it slips, give a second squeeze at 90° to the first. Never crush it flat, as that nicks the line and leaves a weak spot.
